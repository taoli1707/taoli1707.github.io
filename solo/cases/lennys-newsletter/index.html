Overview

Lenny's Newsletter is a weekly Substack publication on product management and growth, with a paid tier, a podcast funded by sponsors, and a paid-subscriber bundle of software tools. Rachitsky told CNBC in January 2024 that newsletter and podcast together brought in a little over $1M a year; he passed 1 million subscribers in 2025.

Origin

Rachitsky was a product lead at Airbnb for about seven years and left in 2019 without planning a media business. He wrote a Medium post on lessons from Airbnb, then started a free newsletter answering readers' product questions. After about ten months free, he launched a paid tier in 2020 and had ~450 paid subscribers and ~$56K ARR six weeks in.

How it makes money

Paid subscriptions (historically $15/month or $150/year, with a higher 'expense it' tier), podcast sponsorships, and since 2025 an annual-plan bundle ('Product Pass') giving paid subscribers free years of tools like Notion, Linear and Granola, which drives annual upgrades.

What drove growth

  • Wrote deeply researched posts from his own Airbnb experience and from surveys of other operators
  • Moved most content behind the paywall while keeping some free issues, pushing conversion
  • Added a podcast with well-known product leaders, which also reached YouTube and brought sponsors
  • Built a paid-subscriber Slack community as a retention perk
  • Negotiated software bundles for annual subscribers, making the subscription easy to expense

Moat & risks

What protects it

Brand trust in a defined professional niche, a large list, and relationships that let him book top guests and negotiate tool bundles that smaller newsletters can't match.

What could hurt it

Depends on one author's output and on Substack's platform. Tech layoffs or budget cuts can reduce the number of subscriptions paid by employers.

Lessons you can reuse

  1. Run free long enough to learn what readers want, then paywall the core content
  2. A narrow professional audience will pay more than a broad consumer one, and often on a company card
  3. A podcast can be a second revenue stream (sponsors) that also feeds the paid list
  4. Perks from partners can raise the perceived value of a subscription at little cost

Idea to build next

A paid newsletter for a professional role that has no 'Lenny' yet, such as hospital operations managers or clinical research coordinators

Why now: Tech roles are saturated with newsletters, but many well-paid operational roles have little practitioner-written, tactical content, and employers already pay for training.

30-day path to a first paying customer

  1. Publish 4 free posts answering real questions from peers in the role
  2. Share them in LinkedIn and professional association groups to collect the first 500 emails
  3. Survey readers and write one deep template-backed guide as the first paid post
  4. Turn on a $15/month or $150/year paid tier with an 'expense it' note

This is our idea, not the founder's. We haven't validated the demand: talk to 10 potential buyers before you write code.
