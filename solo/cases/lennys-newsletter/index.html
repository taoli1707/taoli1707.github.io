Overview

Lenny's Newsletter is a weekly Substack publication on product management and growth, with a paid tier, a podcast funded by sponsors, and a bundle of free software for annual subscribers. CNBC reported in January 2024 that the newsletter brings in more than $500K a year and the podcast more than $500K, over $1M combined. He passed 1 million subscribers in March 2025.

Origin

Rachitsky spent seven years as a product manager at Airbnb. After leaving, he turned his notes on what he had learned into a Medium post that did very well, wrote a few more, then moved to Substack. After about ten months of free posts he added a paid plan at $15/month; by August 2020 he had about 15,000 free subscribers, 500 paid, and roughly $65K a year before fees.

How it makes money

Paid subscriptions ($15/month or $150/year until February 2025, then $20/month or $200/year, plus a $400/year Insider tier), podcast sponsorships, and since February 2025 a bundle ('Product Pass') giving annual subscribers free years of tools like Notion, Linear and Granola, which drives annual upgrades. Many readers expense the subscription.

What drove growth

  • Wrote deeply researched posts from his own Airbnb experience and from surveys of other operators
  • Kept one free post in the mix while most content sat behind the paywall; he says the newsletter grew almost entirely through word of mouth
  • Added a podcast in 2022 with well-known product leaders, which also reached YouTube and brought sponsors
  • Built a paid-subscriber Slack community as a retention perk
  • Negotiated software bundles for annual subscribers and raised prices for the first time at the same moment

Moat & risks

What protects it

Brand trust in a defined professional niche, a large list, and relationships that let him book top guests and negotiate tool bundles that smaller newsletters can't match.

What could hurt it

Depends on one author's output and on Substack's platform. Tech layoffs or budget cuts can reduce the number of subscriptions paid by employers.

Lessons you can reuse

  1. Run free long enough to learn what readers want, then paywall the core content
  2. A narrow professional audience will pay more than a broad consumer one, and often on a company card
  3. A podcast can be a second revenue stream (sponsors) that also feeds the paid list
  4. Perks from partners can raise the perceived value of a subscription at little cost

Rebuild plan · idea to build next

A paid newsletter for a professional role that has no 'Lenny' yet, such as hospital operations managers or clinical research coordinators

01

What to build

Tech roles are saturated with newsletters, but many well-paid operational roles have little practitioner-written, tactical content, and employers already pay for training.

02

Market

Buyers are practitioners in the role and their employers' training budgets, reached through LinkedIn, professional associations and conference communities. Competition comes from trade associations' journals (e.g. ACRP or HFMA publications) and vendor-run blogs, not from established independent newsletters.

03

30-day path to a first paying customer

  1. Publish 4 free posts answering real questions from peers in the role
  2. Share them in LinkedIn and professional association groups to collect the first 500 emails
  3. Survey readers and write one deep template-backed guide as the first paid post
  4. Turn on a $15/month or $150/year paid tier with an 'expense it' note
04

Tech stack

  • Substack or beehiiv
  • Stripe
  • Typeform (reader surveys)
  • Slack or Circle (community)
  • Riverside (podcast)
05

Revenue model

Start with a $15/month or $150/year paid tier and a group plan for teams that expense it, then add sponsorships once the free list passes about 10,000.

This plan is our idea, not the founder's, and the market notes are our judgment. We haven't validated the demand: talk to 10 potential buyers before you write code.
