Overview

Patrick McKenzie (patio11) built a narrow tool for teachers who make bingo cards and published its sales, profit and marketing experiments every year. He started it in 2006 while working as a salaryman in Japan. It grew into a profitable, mostly automated side business that peaked at about $65K in sales in 2012, and he sold it through FE International in 2015.

Origin

McKenzie released Bingo Card Creator 1.0 on July 1, 2006 as a downloadable Java app, after about 47 hours of work. Teachers made bingo cards for vocabulary and lessons by hand, and he automated that job. From 2009 it ran almost entirely as a Rails web app, which let him A/B test the funnel.

How it makes money

A one-time purchase (raised from $24.95 to $29.95 in 2009) after a free trial, with sales from elementary school teachers and strong seasonal spikes around holidays. Historically it ran at about a 60% profit margin.

What drove growth

  • Long-tail SEO. He made one landing page per narrow topic (specific holiday or vocabulary bingo cards), later written by freelancers at scale. He says that content grew to about 50% of sales and 75% of profits.
  • Conversion optimization. He wrote A/Bingo, an open-source Rails A/B testing library, and tested copy, landing pages and the purchase flow.
  • A 2012 redesign and Stripe card payments lifted trial-to-purchase conversion from 1.8% to 2.4%. He called them the best thing that happened to the business in years.
  • The business ran in maintenance mode about 48 weeks a year, so his time went into marketing systems rather than features.

Moat & risks

What protects it

Years of accumulated long-tail pages and search rankings in a niche too small for bigger companies to target. The product itself is easy to copy. The distribution is not.

What could hurt it

Google algorithm changes, and free alternatives or template sites. The seasonal teacher market has limited room to grow; sales were roughly flat in 2011, and he eventually sold mainly to focus on his other businesses.

Lessons you can reuse

  1. Pick a narrow, unglamorous job for a specific user (teachers making bingo cards) where competition is weak.
  2. Programmatic long-tail content, one page per micro-query, can outperform head-term SEO for small products.
  3. Instrument everything and A/B test the funnel. Checkout and payment changes can matter more than features.
  4. Publish your numbers every year. The transparency built him an audience that later paid for consulting and courses.

Rebuild plan · idea to build next

A generator for printable, standards-aligned classroom activities (word searches, bingo, flashcards) that takes a teacher's own vocabulary list and aligns to phonics or ESL curricula, sold to homeschoolers and ESL tutors.

01

What to build

Generic worksheet sites don't map to specific curricula or learner levels. LLMs now make it cheap to produce structured, level-appropriate content from a list, but it needs a strict template layer to print well.

02

Market

Buyers are homeschooling parents and independent ESL/phonics tutors, reached through programmatic SEO pages, Pinterest, homeschool Facebook groups and tutor communities. Competitors are Teachers Pay Teachers, Twinkl, Education.com and free generators like Canva and Wordwall.

03

30-day path to a first paying customer

  1. Pick one curriculum (e.g. a popular phonics sequence or CEFR A1-A2 ESL) and build the generator for it
  2. Programmatically publish 200 free landing pages, one per lesson or word list, each with a sample PDF
  3. Put printing behind a $5/mo or $39/yr plan and post in 3 homeschool or ESL tutor communities
  4. A/B test the paywall copy and checkout from day one
04

Tech stack

  • Next.js
  • Postgres
  • OpenAI API
  • React-PDF or Typst (print templates)
  • Stripe
05

Revenue model

Freemium generator with watermark-free printing on a $5/mo or $39/yr plan, plus a $99/yr multi-student license for tutors and co-ops.

This plan is our idea, not the founder's, and the market notes are our judgment. We haven't validated the demand: talk to 10 potential buyers before you write code.
