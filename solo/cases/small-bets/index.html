Overview

Daniel Vassallo left a ~$500K/yr Amazon Web Services job and ran several small products instead of one startup: an AWS book, a $25 Twitter audience course, a $375 cohort course, and the Small Bets community with a one-time lifetime fee. Small Bets made $824,409 revenue in its first two years, and he sold it to Gumroad for $3.6M in April 2025.

Origin

After leaving AWS, Vassallo co-wrote 'The Good Parts of AWS' (launched on Gumroad on Dec 25, 2019 at $65) and in April 2020 released a $25 video course, 'Everyone Can Build a Twitter Audience', that took about 16 hours to make. He then ran a $375 two-week Small Bets cohort course over Zoom, and gave students lifetime access to a Discord. That Discord became the Small Bets community in November 2021.

How it makes money

Mainly one-time payments for lifetime membership to Small Bets (live events from members and guest speakers, community chat on Discord, recordings, course deals). The lifetime price has moved between about $185 and $245 with Black Friday promotions, and a one-year $99 option was added in November 2023. Earlier income came from the $25 Twitter course and the AWS book.

What drove growth

  • Leaving a ~$500K/yr job to work independently gave him a story that drew early attention
  • Shared exact revenue and hours per product publicly on X, which became marketing
  • The $25 Twitter course (13,000+ sales, ~$310K) proved his audience would buy
  • The $375 cohort course sold out week after week ($150K from 400 customers in 5 months) and seeded the community
  • Paid members and guest experts (typically $1,000 a session) to teach live events, adding content without him producing it all
  • Ran pricing experiments on the lifetime fee, especially on Black Friday; November 2023 was the first $100K revenue month
  • Word of mouth and a small affiliate program (20% referral fee)

Moat & risks

What protects it

Community network effects (thousands of lifetime members and member-run events) plus Vassallo's personal following and public track record.

What could hurt it

Lifetime pricing means little recurring revenue; income depends on continuous new members. Much of the value was tied to the founder, which is why the Gumroad deal requires him to keep running it for five years.

Lessons you can reuse

  1. Measure each product by profit per hour invested, not MRR
  2. A cheap, fast product (a 16-hour video course) can validate an audience before a larger bet
  3. Communities scale when members teach each other, not just the founder
  4. Transparency about numbers can be the marketing for a business-advice product
  5. A lifetime-fee community can still be sold if membership and content are documented

01

What to build

Most indie-business communities assume tech workers; government and nonprofit staff face specific conflict-of-interest rules and have no dedicated space.

02

Market

Buyers are early-career public servants and NGO staff who want permitted side income, reached through LinkedIn, sector newsletters and alumni networks of public-policy schools. Competitors are general indie-business communities such as Small Bets and Indie Hackers, plus free Reddit and Facebook groups.

03

30-day path to a first paying customer

  1. Write one public post about your own side income under those constraints
  2. Run a free live session on what is and isn't allowed, collect emails
  3. Offer a $99 lifetime founding membership with 4 scheduled live classes
  4. Invite 2 members to teach classes on their own side projects

Revenue model

One-time lifetime membership at $99 for founding members, raised in steps toward $199-$249 as the recorded class library grows, with an optional $49/year tier.

This plan is our idea, not the founder's, and the market notes are our judgment. We haven't validated the demand: talk to 10 potential buyers before you write code.
