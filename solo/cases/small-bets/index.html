Overview

Daniel Vassallo left a ~$500K Amazon job in 2019 and ran several small products instead of one startup: a $25 Twitter audience course, an AWS book, and the Small Bets community with a one-time lifetime fee. Small Bets made $824,409 revenue in its first two years; he sold it to Gumroad for $3.6M in April 2025.

Origin

Vassallo quit AWS in 2019 and wrote a widely shared post explaining why, which gave him his first few thousand followers. In April 2020 he recorded a 100-minute webcam course on building a Twitter audience in about 16 hours and sold it on Gumroad for $25. He launched Small Bets in November 2021 as a paid community for people following the same approach.

How it makes money

One-time payments: a lifetime membership to Small Bets (live classes from members and guests, community chat, recordings), plus the earlier $25 Twitter course and an AWS book. No recurring subscription.

What drove growth

  • The 'why I quit a $500K job' post drove initial attention
  • Shared exact revenue and hours per product publicly on X, which became marketing
  • The $25 Twitter course (13,000+ sales, ~$310K) proved his audience would buy
  • Recruited members to teach classes inside Small Bets, adding content without him producing it all
  • Raised the lifetime price over time, creating urgency to join early

Moat & risks

What protects it

Community network effects (thousands of lifetime members and member-run classes) plus Vassallo's personal following and public track record.

What could hurt it

Lifetime pricing means no recurring revenue; income depends on continuous new members. Much of the value was tied to the founder, which the sale to Gumroad had to address.

Lessons you can reuse

  1. Measure each product by profit per hour invested, not MRR
  2. A cheap, fast product (a 16-hour webcam course) can validate an audience before a larger bet
  3. Communities scale when members teach each other, not just the founder
  4. Transparency about numbers can be the marketing for a business-advice product
  5. A lifetime-fee community can still be sold if membership and content are documented

Rebuild plan · idea to build next

What to build

Most indie-business communities assume tech workers; government and nonprofit staff face specific conflict-of-interest rules and have no dedicated space.

02

30-day path to a first paying customer

  1. Write one public post about your own side income under those constraints
  2. Run a free live session on what is and isn't allowed, collect emails
  3. Offer a $99 lifetime founding membership with 4 scheduled live classes
  4. Invite 2 members to teach classes on their own side projects

This plan is our idea, not the founder's, and the market notes are our judgment. We haven't validated the demand: talk to 10 potential buyers before you write code.
