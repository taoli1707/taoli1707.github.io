Overview

Carrd is a builder for single-page websites such as personal profiles, landing pages and link-in-bio pages. It has a free tier and cheap annual Pro plans. AJ, a pseudonymous developer, built it alone, reached $1M ARR with 2.5M sites by April 2021 (with a partial VC round and a two-person team), and told Indie Hackers in 2024 it had reached about $2M ARR with no marketing spend.

Origin

AJ was already known for HTML5 UP, a set of free responsive site templates, and its paid sibling Pixelarity, which gave him a Twitter following. Looking for a bigger challenge, he launched Carrd publicly in March 2016 as 'a free platform for building simple, fully responsive one-page sites for pretty much anything'. His first users and first $100 came from Twitter, and his first 1,000 users and $1,000 from Product Hunt.

How it makes money

Freemium, billed annually only: Pro Lite $9/yr, Pro Standard $19/yr and Pro Plus $49/yr. Higher tiers unlock custom domains, forms, embeds and more sites. The very low annual price turns a large free user base into a steady stream of small payments.

What drove growth

  • Word of mouth and 'Made with Carrd' branding on free sites act as built-in distribution; AJ says growth came entirely from word of mouth.
  • A signup-free builder flow (you can build a site before creating an account) cut onboarding friction for the Product Hunt launch.
  • Adopted as an 'extended profile' linked from Twitter and other social platforms, a segment larger site builders ignored.
  • Seasonal sales (Oct to Dec 2017, Mar 2018) gave clear revenue bumps, which AJ shared in public stats.
  • Deliberately limited features, keeping the product simple rather than chasing full website-builder parity, with no money spent on marketing.

Moat & risks

What protects it

A very low price anchor ($19/yr) combined with millions of sites and strong brand affinity among young creators makes it hard for competitors to undercut or out-simplify.

What could hurt it

Link-in-bio and AI site generators compete for the same simple-site use case. The low price leaves little room if hosting or support costs rise.

Lessons you can reuse

  1. A tiny annual price can beat monthly SaaS pricing when the free tier is huge and the upgrade is obvious.
  2. Feature restraint is a positioning choice: 'simple one-page' is the product.
  3. An audience from earlier free work (templates) can seed a paid product's launch.
  4. Free-tier branding compounds as a zero-cost acquisition channel.

Rebuild plan · idea to build next

A one-page site builder just for local tradespeople (plumbers, electricians, cleaners) with built-in quote-request forms, service-area maps and Google review embeds, at $29/yr.

01

What to build

General builders force tradespeople through design choices they don't care about. Most need one page that ranks locally and collects quote requests.

02

Market

Buyers are owner-operator tradespeople, reached by cold outreach to Google Maps listings without websites, trade Facebook groups and supplier counters. Competitors are Carrd, Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy's builder and lead platforms like Thumbtack and Angi.

03

30-day path to a first paying customer

  1. Build 3 templates with a quote form that emails and texts the owner.
  2. Hand-build 10 sites for tradespeople found through Google Maps listings that have no website.
  3. Charge $29/yr after a 30-day free trial.
  4. Add 'Built with ...' branding to every free site.
04

Tech stack

  • SvelteKit or Next.js
  • Postgres
  • Cloudflare (hosting and custom domains)
  • Twilio (SMS lead alerts)
  • Stripe
  • Google Business Profile API
05

Revenue model

Free branded page on a subdomain, then $29/yr for a custom domain and quote-form SMS alerts, with a $99 done-for-you setup add-on.

This plan is our idea, not the founder's, and the market notes are our judgment. We haven't validated the demand: talk to 10 potential buyers before you write code.
