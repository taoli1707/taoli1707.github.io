Overview

Storemapper is a store locator widget for brands that sell through retailers. It embeds on Shopify, BigCommerce, WordPress, Squarespace and other sites. Tyler Tringas taught himself to code, built the first version on a long flight, grew it while traveling, and sold it to SureSwift Capital in July 2017. It is the case he later used to coin the term 'micro-SaaS'.

Origin

Tringas spent about nine months learning to code, then built Storemapper's first version on a single long flight to Argentina. He charged $5/mo because the first version was buggy, and had paying customers within about 24 hours.

How it makes money

Monthly subscriptions. It started at $5/mo, and he raised the price 80% to $9/mo for new customers once the product matured, then raised it further in later years. The early low price is his own documented lesson in underpricing.

What drove growth

  • Platform app stores. Listing on Shopify's app store and building integrations for BigCommerce, WordPress and Squarespace put it in front of merchants on fast-growing platforms.
  • A viral footer. The 'powered by Storemapper' link on nearly every customer's locator produced thousands of clicks a month.
  • Organic search for 'store locator' terms. He never ran paid ads.
  • Repeated price increases on new customers raised ARPU without adding more customers.

Moat & risks

What protects it

A small but real set of integrations across ecommerce platforms and embedded widgets that are annoying to replace. The niche was too small for large companies to target.

What could hurt it

Platform dependence: Shopify can ship a native locator or change app-store rules. Competing plugins are cheap, and a low-ARPU product with churn needs constant new customers.

Lessons you can reuse

  1. Build on top of a fast-growing platform's app store. Its growth becomes your distribution.
  2. Ship embedded products with a 'powered by' link so every customer markets for you.
  3. Don't anchor on your launch price. Raise it for new customers once the product works.
  4. A narrow utility can support a traveling solo founder and still be sellable to a SaaS holding company.

Rebuild plan · idea to build next

A 'find our product near you' locator for craft breweries, wineries and specialty food brands that ingests messy distributor depletion reports (CSV/Excel) and keeps retailer locations up to date automatically.

Difficulty
●●●●● Easy
Hours / week
8-12 (fits a side hustle)
Best fit
A data engineer who enjoys messy spreadsheet ETL and geocoding
01

What to build

Generic store locators assume the brand keeps a clean store list. Beverage and food brands selling through distributors get irregular spreadsheets, so their 'where to buy' pages go stale.

02

30-day path to a first paying customer

  1. Collect 5 real distributor reports from brewery owners and write a parser that normalizes and geocodes accounts
  2. Ship an embeddable map widget with Shopify and WordPress snippets
  3. Charge $29/mo with a monthly 'upload your report' email and auto-refresh
  4. Sell directly to 20 local breweries via Instagram DMs and state brewers' guild forums

This plan is our idea, not the founder's, and the market notes are our judgment. We haven't validated the demand: talk to 10 potential buyers before you write code.
