Overview

TypingMind is a chat front-end for ChatGPT, Claude and other models, where users bring their own API keys. Tony Dinh built it alone in March 2023 and sold one-time licenses, later adding cloud-sync and team subscriptions. It passed $500K total revenue in February 2024, and in April 2025 he reported $148K revenue for the previous month, an all-time high, along with his first two hires.

Origin

Dinh, a Vietnamese developer who had already built DevUtils and Black Magic, was annoyed by ChatGPT's slow typing and lack of search. OpenAI released the ChatGPT API on March 1, 2023; he bought typingmind.com on March 2 and shared the first version on X on March 6. He woke up to $428.58 of revenue, and by his own newsletter account it made about $22.7K in the first 7 days.

How it makes money

One-time lifetime licenses for individuals in three tiers (Standard, Extended, Premium). Users pay model providers directly through their own API keys. Subscriptions cover TypingCloud sync and backup and the team product (TypingMind Custom, now documented as TypingMind Teams), which starts at $99/month for 5 seats plus $8 per extra user. In February 2024 the subscription side was at $15K MRR.

What drove growth

  • Launched on X to an existing audience of developers, with daily revenue updates: $1K, $2K, $4K, then $10K by March 10, 2023; he says 99% of early sales came from his Twitter reach.
  • Reached #1 Product of the Day on Product Hunt on March 11, 2023, adding about $5K.
  • Rode the ChatGPT API wave: it gave power users a better UI with prompt libraries, plugins and multi-model chat, without a ChatGPT Plus subscription.
  • Added support for each new model and feature (web search, image generation, artifacts, MCP) quickly after release.
  • Moved upmarket with a team version, which adds recurring B2B revenue on top of one-time licenses.
  • Ran an affiliate program until January 2025, then replaced it with a referral program that rewards cloud storage.

Moat & risks

What protects it

The moat is speed and polish more than technology: a large installed base of license holders, a plugin ecosystem, and B2B team deployments that are sticky once rolled out.

What could hurt it

Model providers keep improving their own chat apps, which shrinks the gap a third-party UI fills. One-time licenses cap lifetime value unless the team and cloud tiers keep growing.

Lessons you can reuse

  1. When a new platform API ships, the first good UI on top of it can win a big share of early adopters.
  2. Bring-your-own-key pricing removes your biggest variable cost, so a one-time license can be very profitable.
  3. Sell one-time to individuals and recurring to teams, so each segment pays the way it prefers.
  4. Share revenue screenshots early: the first-day numbers were themselves the launch marketing.

What to build

General chat UIs target individuals and big enterprises. Small regulated firms want to use AI but need redaction and logging they can show to insurers or regulators.

02

Market

Buyers are managing partners and office managers at small law, accounting and medical practices, reached through LinkedIn, bar and CPA associations, and IT consultants who serve those firms. Competitors include TypingMind, ChatGPT Team/Enterprise, Microsoft Copilot and vertical tools such as Harvey and CoCounsel.

03

30-day path to a first paying customer

  1. Fork or build a simple chat UI with a regex and NER PII-redaction layer and a local audit log export.
  2. Write 20 prompts for one profession (e.g. small law firms) with a practitioner.
  3. Offer a $299 one-time license plus a $29/mo 'compliance pack' to 20 firms reached through LinkedIn and local bar associations.
  4. Collect testimonials and publish a one-page compliance explainer.
