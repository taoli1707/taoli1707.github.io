Overview

Josh W. Comeau, a front-end developer known for his interactive blog, left his job at Gatsby in 2020 and built a CSS course on his own platform. A one-week early-access pre-sale in March 2021 brought in about $550K against a $50K goal. He later released a second course, The Joy of React (2023).

Origin

Comeau worked at Khan Academy, DigitalOcean, Concordia University and Gatsby Inc. (as a Staff Software Engineer). After a repetitive stress injury he left his job in summer 2020 and started the course in August. Over 2020 he grew from about 10k Twitter followers and 1k email subscribers to 31k followers and about 28k subscribers, helped by viral blog posts and a free book. With the course roughly half done, he opened a one-week pre-sale on March 10-16, 2021.

How it makes money

One-time purchase with tiered packages (Basic, Pro and Ultimate), regional purchasing-power-parity pricing, team licenses and a bundle with The Joy of React. The 2021 early-access price was $129 (expected full price $349).

What drove growth

  • Wrote interactive blog posts with live demos that spread on Twitter and developer forums; his most popular posts topped 100k visits
  • Converted Twitter followers into a newsletter before launch, including a free book (about 8k sign-ups) and an animated sign-up prompt on the blog
  • Built in public on Twitter from late 2020, sharing GIFs of the platform and content
  • Ran a one-week pre-sale at an early-access discount; the $50K target was hit in about 10 minutes
  • Built a custom learning platform with articles, videos, exercises, workshops and mini-games that set the course apart from video-only courses
  • Reused the platform and list for a second course, The Joy of React (2023)

Moat & risks

What protects it

High production quality and interactive content on a custom platform, plus a trusted personal brand among front-end developers.

What could hurt it

Launch-driven revenue is lumpy. AI coding assistants may reduce how much developers feel they need to learn CSS fundamentals.

Lessons you can reuse

  1. A year of free, high-quality teaching content builds the trust that makes a pre-sale work
  2. Pre-selling a half-finished course funds the rest and validates price
  3. Owning the platform allows interactive formats competitors on generic course hosts can't match
  4. Set a modest target; a realistic goal makes the decision to keep going clear

What to build

Many business staff now query data with AI help but lack fundamentals to check results; most SQL courses target developers or data scientists.

02

Market

Buyers are product managers, ops and marketing analysts at non-tech companies, often on a training budget, reached through LinkedIn posts, SEO on interactive articles and a newsletter. Competitors include DataCamp, Mode's SQL tutorial, Codecademy, Udemy courses and free AI assistants.

03

30-day path to a first paying customer

  1. Publish 3 interactive blog posts with in-browser SQL playgrounds on common business questions
  2. Collect emails with a free 'SQL mistakes that give wrong numbers' checklist
  3. Pre-sell the first 3 modules at an early-access price for one week
  4. Deliver module 1 within 2 weeks of the pre-sale
04

Tech stack

  • Next.js
  • MDX
  • DuckDB-WASM or sql.js (in-browser SQL)
  • ConvertKit (Kit)
  • Stripe or Lemon Squeezy

Revenue model

One-time purchase with a ~$99 early-access pre-sale, then $199-$349 tiers at full launch, plus team licenses.

This plan is our idea, not the founder's, and the market notes are our judgment. We haven't validated the demand: talk to 10 potential buyers before you write code.
