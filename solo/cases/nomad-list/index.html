Overview

Nomad List ranks cities for remote workers on cost of living, internet speed, weather and safety, and sold paid membership to a community of nomads. Pieter Levels started it in 2014 as a crowdsourced spreadsheet and has run it mostly alone ever since; it was renamed Nomads.com in 2024. In September 2024 he listed it at $61K/month in a revenue breakdown. In September 2026 he made it almost free ($1 to sign up) after 43,252 paid members over 12 years, betting on sponsors instead.

Origin

In 2014 Levels was working from Chiang Mai on a small income and had started a challenge to launch 12 startups in 12 months. He tweeted a public Google Spreadsheet asking people to add internet speeds, cost of living and other data on cities good for remote work, and it filled up quickly. He then turned the sheet into a website, which became Nomad List.

How it makes money

For most of its life, paid membership unlocked the community (chat, meetups, dating and member features) and full city data; the price and billing model changed many times. Sponsorship (a long-running one with SafetyWing) also contributed. In September 2026 Levels cut the price to a $1 anti-spam signup fee and said sponsors will likely be the future model.

What drove growth

  • The crowdsourced spreadsheet validated demand before any code was written, and contributors became the first audience.
  • SEO: thousands of city and comparison pages ('cost of living in X') bring in steady organic search traffic.
  • Open-startup transparency: Levels published live revenue and user dashboards from February 2018, which fed press coverage and his own following.
  • Conversion work: he reported sign-ups up 26% after switching Nomad List to Stripe Checkout in 2021.
  • Combined with Remote OK, he reported $83,333+/mo (a $1M/yr run rate) in May 2019 with 0 ad budget and 0 funding.

Moat & risks

What protects it

More than a decade of crowdsourced city data and reviews, strong SEO rankings, and a paid community with network effects are hard to replicate quickly.

What could hurt it

Revenue swings with travel trends and the remote-work cycle, and dropping the paid membership in 2026 makes it dependent on sponsors. The product depends on one founder who now spreads his attention across many projects.

Lessons you can reuse

  1. Validate with a spreadsheet before writing code: a public sheet can prove demand in a day.
  2. Programmatic data pages (one per city, one per comparison) compound SEO for years.
  3. Monetize the community, not just the data. Public data brings people in and membership paid the bills for a decade.
  4. Keep infrastructure tiny. Levels reported ~$5,450/mo in total costs for both sites in 2019, and 1 VPS.

Rebuild plan · idea to build next

A 'where to live' data site plus paid community for remote-working families: school options, pediatric care, kid-friendly neighborhoods and visa rules for dependents, city by city.

01

What to build

Nomad-focused sites are built around single travellers. Families relocating for remote work have different, higher-stakes data needs and are willing to pay more.

02

Market

Buyers are remote-working parents planning a move abroad, reached through expat-family Facebook groups, subreddits and SEO on 'moving to X with kids'. Competitors are Nomads.com, InterNations, Expatica and relocation consultants.

03

30-day path to a first paying customer

  1. Start a public Google Sheet of 30 cities with columns for international schools, fees and pediatric hospitals, and post it in remote-family Facebook groups and subreddits.
  2. Interview 10 families who moved abroad and write 5 in-depth city guides from those interviews.
  3. Ship a static site from the sheet with one page per city.
  4. Sell a $49/year membership that includes a private chat and monthly city deep-dive calls.
04

Tech stack

  • Astro or Next.js (static city pages)
  • Postgres or SQLite
  • Stripe
  • Circle or Discourse (community)
  • Google Sheets (seed data)
05

Revenue model

Keep city pages free for SEO and charge a $49/year family membership for the community and deep-dive calls, with paid relocation-consultation add-ons at around $150 per call.

This plan is our idea, not the founder's, and the market notes are our judgment. We haven't validated the demand: talk to 10 potential buyers before you write code.
